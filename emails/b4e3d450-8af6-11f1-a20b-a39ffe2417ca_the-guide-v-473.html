+ Shaun J. Wright Brings a Lifetime of House Music, Performance and Purpose [Ultramaroon] "The state of house music is as complex as it probably has always been. There has been a larger industry component around it as a commodity over the last 20 years. It is not underground anymore. There are underground components, but what we see in the mainstream does not really reflect its roots. The face of house music is no longer Black or queer, but the sound is. I find that complex and something I have to wrestle with philosophically.

Black American music forms repeatedly create something without necessarily being able to live in it or benefit from it.

We still have people from the time the music was made who can communicate with us. I wish more of their stories were told."
+ R.I.P. +

+ John 'Jammin' Collins, influential Detroit techno DJ and "ambassador", has died [A longtime champion of Detroit techno, Collins was closely associated with Underground Resistance and supported the city’s scene as a DJ, mentor, and advocate] [DJ Mag] Speaking to Marke Bieschke in 2025 for a feature exploring the queer roots of Detroit techno, Collins, who became known to many as the "Ambassador of Techno", recalled this period, in which adventurous musical curation and genre fusion harmonised with open-minded audiences of all backgrounds:

“The crowd was very eclectic — gay, straight, Black, white, suburbs, downtown — and back then you played the whole night — five, six hours. That took a lot of records! [...] It’s interesting to look back at those days now. It was somehow simpler. I was just happy to be earning my $45 a night, a little gay DJ doing his thing.”
