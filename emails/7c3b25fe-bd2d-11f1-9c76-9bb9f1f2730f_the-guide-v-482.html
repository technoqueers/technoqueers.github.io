+ The 'Last Ditch Bar Mask-Optional Drama' +

+ A Western Mass. lesbian bar required KN-95 masks. When it changed the rules, all hell broke loose. [Boston Globe] “Just say you’re being complicit in a mass disabling event and get it over with,” wrote one commenter. “People like you kill disabled people in the name of your own leisure and convenience,” posted another. “I hope your venue gets firebombed for robbing my fellow immunocompromised people of their Saturday plans just to scrape extra revenue from ablists [sic],” wrote a third.

Two days later, Last Ditch posted an apology. Instead of defusing the situation, the apology seemed to make things worse. The bar had erred: it posted an Instagram card without providing alt-text for the visually impaired. This set off a fresh round of accusations of ableism.

"There's conflict, but we just hope that there's more repair than conflict. That's the price you pay to be in community."

+ The Social Justice Pyramid Scheme [Is it activism, or emotional manipulation?] [Mental Hellth] "While the story was humorous, I was struck by the real harm buried within it: a community space for queer people was ruined, and people were harmed: Jackie Matellian, one of the owners of the bar, ended up homeless and living in her truck for six months, and tens of thousands of dollars in debt in an attempt to appease everyone and keep the bar open under its strict yet ever-shifting rules of “inclusion” (rules that actually drove people away from the space). 

Her incessant appeasement did not work. Revealing to her cohort that she was in debt resulted in accusations of “centering her feelings” above others."

+ Lesbian Bar Project Co-Founder: Purity Politics Could Destroy Our Remaining Spaces [Uncloseted] "Due to the constraints of purity politics—some in good faith, some not so much—lesbian bar owners, almost all of whom are queer women themselves, are forced to be everything for everyone, similarly to how women are forced to be everything for everyone. By asking these bars to uphold impossible standards—standards very few other third spaces are asked to consider—we all end up losing."

+ Is it just me, or does the story of the COVID-conscious Western Mass lesbian bar circular firing squad led by the he/him lesbian racial justice revolutionary and the nonbinary secular hijab Irish rights identitarian that bankrupted a woman $50k in debt on her way to clown college slightly remind you of the COVID-millenarian Reed College queer polycule solitary confinement DIY hamster zine communication network turned skin-bleached animal cruelty survivalists that were forced to reconvene and infect each other in a jail cell? [bradtroemel]

+ "My most chud LGBTQ opinion is that it's not healthy for gay and lesbian nightlife to be too sex-segregated. Gay bar with no lesbians = racism and meth. Lesbian bar with no gays = no fun allowed ever. Stonewall was a mixed crowd and we are all better for it." [Trans Spirit, Trans Soul]

+ Photography +

+ Portraits of gay bears through a rooftop hatch [Huck] "Gay culture has become much more expansive in the ways it talks about sexuality, gender and masculinity, but body representation can still feel remarkably narrow."

"I wanted to photograph these men without asking them to symbolise anything. No transformations. No before-and-after narrative. No argument about what a bear should weigh, wear or look like. Just portraits of men I think are beautiful, made in the place where my life with many of them actually happens."

+ ‘You’ve got to be pretty tough to be a drag queen out there’: Damien Frost’s immaculate queer portraits [Guardian] "Whether he photographs people on a farm or at a warehouse party, Frost is most interested in how people use fashion, makeup and prosthetics to explore their identity. “I’m not looking for people in fancy dress, I’m looking for people who are expressing something,” he says. Sometimes, this goes beyond experiments with glamour, style or gender. “A lot of the photos I take, the characters can be quite grotesque. Often, it’s people dressing up as a manifestation of how they feel they’ve been treated … they’re made to feel like a monster, so they reclaim that.”

+ One of America’s most important restaurants is closing. It feels personal. [At Lil’ Deb’s Oasis in Hudson, New York, food critic Elazar Sontag found queer community and a sense of purpose that shaped his life.] [WaPo] "The magic animating their operation was their staff, a band of mostly queer cooks and artists who were encouraged to use the space as a stage for ideas that had nowhere else to go. There were spirited drag shows (more than one server moonlighted as a performer), art installations on the walls and, thanks to one particularly talented designer-slash-server, some of the best merch anywhere, including a “Thank You for Being So Hot!” T-shirt that I still get compliments on and an “If U Gay, Perfect” bumper sticker lifted from a disgruntled online review."
