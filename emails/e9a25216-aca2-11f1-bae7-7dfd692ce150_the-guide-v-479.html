+ Grime Square +

+ The Replicable Carnival [Geoffrey Mak’s ‘Rave Methods’ Lecture Pays Attention to Raving] [Grime Square] "Mak referred to the industrial qualities of techno as dystopic. I disagreed: it’s an understatement. Nowadays, a former factory, neighbors a radioactive dumpsite and a graveyard. Raves often occur in the remains of work and production. A local rave is apocalyptic. One dances away where things have already ended, dances near resting places, dances near the half-life thorium remains. An apocalypse is the end of one thing, the beginning of another. Whatever’s coming next happens among corpses."

+ Signalmixer [East Williamsburg Fights Tech With Techno] [Grime Square] "Electronic music idealizes the most optimistic parts of technology. It’s captured in the cult. We see only the worst of ourselves in the tech elite: relentless striving, the desire for validation, an idealism that fits only one. Every attack against tech culture further reifies their cultural power. Every small resistance is a sign that we’ve already lost, but it’s embarrassing to do nothing.

Joy is not an act of resistance, but rage is. Matching Alo Set Americans should walk around the Williamsburg made just for them knowing they’re despised. Their unfulfillment is a beautiful thing."

+ Our Disgruntled Correspondents: Enough With The Noise [A reader sends in footage from the loudness wars on Wyckoff Avenue] [Grime Square] "The natives aren’t the ones complaining. It’s the gentrifying transplants making noise complaints. If they don’t like the culture in Bushwick, they can easily pack up & go back to the same Ohio suburb that they came from."
+ Gentrification +

+ Mykki Blanco Doesn’t Want To See You Standing in Line for a Croissant [Interview] "NYC needs to get mean again, exclusive and bitchy. Everyone got nice and the city is overrun with lames now. There is nothing wrong with a little elitism. No one wants to be a snob anymore and that’s why you have transplants embarrassing everyone waiting in hour-long lines for croissants."

+ Can a Rock Club Survive in North Brooklyn? [A young person used to be able to stumble along Kent Avenue into live music. Now, nowhere can stay open.] [Curbed] "When Music Hall of Williamsburg opened in 2007, the original places that had made the scene began to disappear. Glasslands, Death by Audio, and 285 Kent have all closed. Barcade, which opened in 2004, recently announced plans to exit Williamsburg after its lease expires; the bar’s management said they know “the end is coming” because “nearly all the brick and wood buildings around us have been demolished and rebuilt in steel and glass, and the nightlife has mostly faded away.”

“Live music does not work at 400 bucks a foot. Few things do. You’ve got to be selling very expensive, very nice stuff.”

+ How Williamsburg Lost Its Swag [A new generation of gentrifiers is gentrifying the old gentrifiers] [Slate] "Amazon Music and A.I.-focused ecommerce startups Queen One and Autonomous Technologies all set up shop at a Williamsburg waterfront building with floor-to-ceiling glass windows. So did A.I. software firm Rillavoice, and Altana AI, which has a two-year contract with the U.S. Customs and Border Protection to enforce tariffs (a job that is definitely not evil and sounds super awesome). The landmark Domino Sugar factory was gut-renovated and now serves as offices for over 30 A.I. and tech firms."

+ The Politics of Dancing +

+ Girlbossing the Rave [How lack of transparency and founder-first branding fractured the Support Women DJs community] [Politics of Dancing] "A number of members share the view that Brosman started the collective with good intentions, but became more business-focussed and less community-minded as the project expanded. “I think it flipped as soon as she realised she couldn’t make a lot of money from ‘doing the right thing’.”

"Support Women DJs is a small but really important part of the capitalist extraction of culture. Organizations like theirs, their function is really to absorb the revolutionary potential of the DJ community, make it apolitical and sell it back to us so we get a taste of what it feels like to be part of something positive. Then they shield themselves entirely from any accountability, so they're not really building any community but they're profiting from the idea that they are."

+ Books +

+ Cruising: Gay Matchbooks [Dark Entries] "A love letter to a lost art, Cruising: Gay Matchbooks (second edition) is an archive of 200 matchbooks from gay bars and bathhouses. The book includes matchbooks from iconic institutions including The Saint, Florent, The Stud, St. Marks Baths and The Eagle, as well as lesser-known spots across the globe. The archive is brought to life with 12 essays from leading voices on gay culture."

+ SMUT PRESS on Parties, Publishing and Throwing an Afters for Robyn [Jack Scollard and Jordan Hearns publish books about cruising grounds and queer intimacy, then throw parties to fund the next one. Four years and several raves later, SMUT Press is one of the most distinctive forces in the UK party scene.] [Notion] 

"Queer venues closing has more to do with bars and pubs and how expensive it’s been, wages not going up in the UK. Clubbing is a slightly different experience; you still need some financial resources but it does not require the same costs to participate like a bar or pub does. Where we do parties, they’re not speciﬁcally queer spaces, but when we do it, it is. Now, more so than when we moved to London ﬁve years ago, there’s more diversity of queer club nights than ever. Running a venue is very different from running a club night — a lot more overhead risk than doing one every three months."

+ Berlin +

+ WHOLE Marks A Pivotal Chapter With “UHaul Dyke Rescue” Takeover [Cold] "Uhaul Dyke Rescue is an emergency rescue service sent from the future to make space for the dykes today – a time in which we’ve lost most of our gay bars, queer rights are rolling backwards and we need queer community, collective joy and a little silliness more than ever.

I came up with the idea when I was doing a Masters in Interior Design. My final project was designing the Mobile Dyke Bar, a queer venue in a van that pops up wherever it’s needed. After graduating I decided to create it for real as a touring festival venue, and I put a callout for handydykes to help me build it in 2024. We’ve since taken the Mobile Dyke Bar everywhere from Glastonbury and Field Maneuvers to the V&A."
