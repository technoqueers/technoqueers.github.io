+ Doll Invasion +

+ How Doll Invasion Went From a Trans House Party to a Fire Island Mainstay [Vogue] "Each room hosted two or more people, none of whom belonged to the island's main demographic. Instead, they were Black and brown art fags, women, and people I'd describe as punk-of-center. Gays were in the kitchen cackling, dykes were bringing soft drugs, and the cross-dressers held court in a hot tub that had never seen so many top surgery scars."

"The event’s name was an homage to the island’s annual Drag Invasion. As the story goes, in 1976, Teri Warren, a drag artist who identifies as a trans woman today, went to have dinner at a space now occupied by the Blue Whale restaurant, but she was denied entry. This was at a time when labels like “drag queen,” “cross-dresser,” “transvestite,” and “transsexual” were used more or less interchangeably, and even in the Pines—a community already at the margins of society—those on the margins of the margins often got the boot.

But Teri wouldn’t stand for it. She told all her friends (who were pissed, by the way) and on the Fourth of July, Teri got all her girls together in head-to-toe glam and took a ferry to the Pines, where they descended on the crowded restaurant in fierce fantasy fashion. Thus, the “Drag Invasion” was born."

+ R.I.P. +

+ Gateway Drag: Tim Curry’s Illicit Gifts to Queer Children [Artforum] "The ’90s were a rough time for kids seeking nontoxic models of masculinity. There was not yet a manosphere, because many of its principles were acceptably mainstream. In that wasteland, Curry was an antidote. He was effervescently queer in a way that felt powerful and compelling enough to challenge straight hegemony. He leaned so far into his cartoonish villainy that he came out the other end as a heroic beacon for all the weirdo children looking not just to understand themselves but to become something glitteringly and magnificently other."
