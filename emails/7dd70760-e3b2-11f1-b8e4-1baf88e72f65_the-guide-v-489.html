+ Toronto +

+ Underground Resistance presents Drexciya and beyond [All You Can Dig] "Underground Resistance is a collective started by “Mad” Mike Banks and Jeff Mills in 1989 in Detroit. The ethos of the organization was founded on principles similar to those of workers’ unions, including the United Auto Workers (UAW), to act as a vestige for working class Black Detroiters at a time when divestment in the city and its communities was high. The beginning lines of their manifesto anchor the collective’s spirit: 

“Underground Resistance is a label for a movement. A movement that wants change by sonic revolution.”
 
In the 1990s, Banks brought a Detroit-based duo called Drexciya (Gerald Donald and James Stinson) to UR. As Drexciya, Gerald and James, along with others, created a sonic and visual orthography that imagined an underwater realm born of the Middle Passage and unfolded in Drexciya’s music and within the liner notes of multiple UR album releases.

The myth of Drexciya has come to life through associated visual artists, notably Detroit-based artists Frankie C. Fultz, who designed the original album artwork for The Return of Drexciya (1996), and AbuQadim Haqq, who designed the original album artwork for Neptune’s Lair (1999) and the graphic novel The Book of Drexciya.

Until December 20, Underground Resistance presents the sonic and visual world of Drexciya at the fourth edition of the Toronto Biennial of Art."

+ In Kazakhstan, queer communities dance in the face of oppression [Dazed] "Widely considered central Asia’s queer capital, Almaty, Kazakhstan’s largest city, hosts boisterous drag shows like those at Blush Bar, LGBTQ techno nights, gay saunas, voguing ballrooms and lesbian-only, anti-patriarchal meet-ups. For many in the majority-Muslim but largely secular nation, which legalised homosexuality in 1998 after gaining post-Soviet independence, Almaty has become a kind of queer mecca.

But this year, things have taken a darker turn: a new law that criminalizes LGBTQ+ "propaganda" came into force. It's now illegal to disseminate info about "non-traditional sexual orientation" if it has the goal of "forming a positive societal opinion." Any materials featuring LGBTQ+ themes are now required to have warning labels and authorities no longer require judicial authorization to block online content.

The Kazakh law forms part of a wider trend of anti-LGBTQ+ policies across eastern Europe and central Asia that experts say has been encouraged by Russia and the Trump administration, which cut financial aid to rights groups in Kazakhstan. [...] In 2024, Georgia passed legislation prohibiting same-sex marriages and civil unions and restricting public displays of LGBTQ symbols."

"Sekerbayeva, who will soon be running queer tours of the city, believes that like the case of post-Orban Hungary, Kazakhstan can return to its previous liberties."

+ How vibes ate the world [Culture Journalist] "From the AI stock market bubble to Governor Kathy Hochul deploying the National Guard to NYC subways based on the “feeling” of danger, it’s clear that the modern world runs increasingly on… vibes. That’s the subject of Good Vibes Only: Phenomenology and The Biopolitics of Algorithmic Legitimation, the new book by writer, philosopher, and returning CUJO guest Robin James. In it, Robin examines how the hidden mathematical systems fueling AI, recommender systems, and other digital technologies are changing how power operates and the futures it makes possible.

Robin joins us to discuss what “vibes” are, how they operate as the cultural manifestation of this shift, and why she believes they have replaced “norms” and other systems of control as a way that white patriarchal capitalism perpetuates itself. We also dig into the shift from musical genres to vibes in the age of streaming, the intersection of emotions with financial markets and algorithmic recommendation systems, and what resistance to this regime might look like."

+ How Do Ordinary People Change the World? As of 2026, the project’s public website has had more than 14 million visits, and its content has been downloaded more than 600,000 times.

AIDS activists were “usually portrayed as cute, white men, young men. We wanted to give women and people of color the opportunity to participate.”

The oral history project was funded through MIX NYC, a non-for-profit organization founded by Schulman and Hubbard that focuses on queer, experimental film."

+ R.I.P. +

+ Joseph Watt, cofounder of esteemed remix service Razormaid, dies [The San Francisco-based service helped shape underground dance music in the '80s and '90s, earning a cult following among DJs] [RA] "Watt founded Razormaid in San Francisco in 1984 alongside Art Maharg. Razormaid became particularly influential for its championing of darker European electronic sounds, including synth pop, industrial and EBM, at a time when the Bay Area's club scene was largely dominated by hi-NRG. Its remixes also reached a wider audience through official commissions for artists including Depeche Mode, Erasure, DAF and Blancmange."

+ Last Dance at Dead Letter No. 9 [Saying goodbye to the wonderfully weird world of a Williamburg club that defied any and all convention] [BK Mag] "To survive, let alone thrive, for three years in the nightlife business—particularly in present-day Williamsburg—is no small feat. To do so without bottle service, exorbitant cover charges, or velvet ropes makes it all the more remarkable.

“I feel like we never meant to open a nightclub. We didn’t come here to do, like, something cool and chic and sceney. It was an art project, and the intentions were really pure.”
